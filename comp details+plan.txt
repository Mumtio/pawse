TRACK DESCRIPTIONS:
The three tracks are: Happiness, Wellness, and Health!

Happiness
This is our beginner track! We expect to see more fun/lighthearted/story-based projects, such as a compliment generator, random acts of kindness bingo, pet a virtual pet stress reliever, etc. Essentially, projects that do not require such a technical knowledge that works with real health data, but would still contribute to a person's wellness if they used it daily/weekly/monthly.

Wellness
This is our intermediate track! We expect to see somewhat technical projects, such as a hydration tracker, mood journal, stretch break timer, gratitude journal, etc. Note: If you make a chatbot designed to improve someones mood, be mindful of the guard rails you put in place; your tool should not be vulnerable to negative talk that would make the user's mood/mental health worse.

Health
This is our advanced track! We expect to see more advanced/technical projects, such as a posture analyzer, voice based mental health screener, medication tracker, etc. (essentially, anything that may be helpful for real health data. It is important to try to add security measures to protect health data to your project, as that would be required for a real life medical system).

Judging Criteria
Wellness Impact
Does the project help users relax, track their health more accurately, or more creatively? Is it useful or inspiring for users? Would you say that your wellness would improve if you used this app daily or weekly?
Creativity & Innovation
Did the team come up with something fresh, original, or unexpected? Ex: New ideas or twists on existing ones, clever ways to use AI to solve problems, Projects that make you go, "Wow, I've never seen that before!"
Technical Craft & Execution
Are the prototypes & demos working as intended? Is there smart usage of AI tools, frameworks, APIs, & clear strategic effort? Projects aren't required to have working backend or hosting, but will be awarded higher technical scores if included.
Design & User Experience
Is it easy & enjoyable to use? Does it look & feel good? Ex: Clean & intuitive design, clear navigation, UI/UX that feels welcoming, polished & accessible. We encourage you to have fun & be creative, but if that hinders UI/UX you may lose points.
Community & Accessibility
Does the project make wellness more inclusive/accessible? Ex: Features that help users from different backgrounds/cultures, efforts to make wellness feel natural/less like a hassle. You may lose points if AI usage hinders the accessibility of the project.




idea:
Pawse: An Agentic Pixel Cat for Focus and Digital Wellness
Track

Wellness — Intermediate Track

One-Line Pitch

Pawse is a privacy-first pixel cat that lives on the corner of the user’s screen, turns real tasks from Notion and assignment files into adventures, protects the user from digital distractions, and encourages healthier work habits through gentle reminders and virtual-pet progression.

The Problem

Students and computer users often struggle with several connected problems:

Unintentional social-media scrolling
Frequent interruptions during focused work
Forgetting tasks and deadlines across different platforms
Difficulty breaking large assignments into manageable steps
Excessive uninterrupted screen time
Forgetting to drink water, stretch or rest their eyes
Productivity applications feeling stressful, mechanical or punitive

Existing productivity tools usually require users to continuously maintain task lists, manually start timers and monitor their own behavior. Website blockers can also be overly restrictive because they block entire platforms even when parts of those platforms are useful.

For example, a student might need YouTube for a tutorial but become distracted by Shorts and recommendations. They might have an assignment written in Notion but forget one of its submission requirements. Even when they use a Pomodoro timer, the experience can feel like another obligation rather than a supportive system.

The Solution

Pawse combines five systems into one application:

An interactive pixel pet
A focus and task-management system
An attention gatekeeper
A Notion-powered task scavenger
A real-task-to-adventure generator

The cat does not simply send notifications. It understands the user’s current task, prepares a suitable focus environment, hides unnecessary distractions, remembers commitments and transforms completed work into progress inside the pet’s world.

The design is supportive rather than punishing. Missing a task never causes the cat to become sick, hungry or upset. The cat rewards progress, recovery and intentional rest without using guilt.

The Pixel Cat

The cat lives inside a small transparent, always-on-top desktop window.

The user can:

Drag it anywhere on the screen
Pet it with the cursor
Pick it up and move it
Feed it
Give it water
Play with it
Dress it in unlocked outfits
Give it toys
Decorate its room
Rename it
Select its personality
Temporarily hide it
Enable quiet or do-not-disturb mode

The cat reacts to the user’s current activity:

It studies while the user works.
It stretches during stretch breaks.
It waters its plants when the user drinks water.
It sleeps when the computer is idle.
It waves after prolonged scrolling.
It celebrates when a focus session finishes.
It puts on glasses when the user reads.
It becomes sleepy during the user’s selected wind-down time.
It carries task reminders onto the screen as small paper scrolls.
It pushes distracting content away during deep-work mode.

Possible personalities include calm, playful, sleepy, chaotic, encouraging and studious.

The Attention Gatekeeper is one of Pawse’s main distinguishing features.

During a focus session, the cat knows what the user intends to accomplish and adjusts the digital environment accordingly.

New-site relevance check

If the user opens a potentially distracting website during focus, the cat asks:

“Does this page help with our current chapter?”

The user can choose:

A page saved for later goes into a local reading list or a Notion “Curiosity Box.”

Gentle anti-doomscrolling

Pawse observes signals such as:

Time spent on a distracting website
Continuous scrolling
Frequent tab switching
Low relevance to the active task
Repeated short-video navigation
Whether leisure time was intentionally started

When the system detects possible unintentional scrolling, it does not immediately block the user. The cat appears and asks:

“Still enjoying this, or did we get stuck scrolling?”

Available actions include:

Continue intentionally
Give me five more minutes
Start a break
Block this website
Return to my task
Save this page for later

In gentle mode, the website may first become grayscale and lose its recommendation feed. In strict mode, the site can be blocked until the focus session ends.

Notification batching

During deep work, Pawse can hold nonurgent application reminders and its own wellness notifications until the next suitable break.

It does not postpone important medication alerts without explicit user settings.

The user chooses exactly which pages Pawse can access.

For example:

Pawse chapter:

When the user completes the chapter, Pawse asks:

“Should I mark ‘Implement semantic error detection’ as complete in Notion?”

The update occurs only after confirmation.

For an initial prototype, Pawse can connect to one shared Notion task database and synchronize manually. A later version can use OAuth, automatic synchronization and multiple workspaces.

Agentic Behaviour

Pawse uses a bounded agentic system.

The cat can:

Examine user-approved information
Identify commitments
Propose task structures
Generate adventures
Prepare a focus environment
Decide when a gentle intervention may help
Select a suitable pet reaction
Suggest changes based on previous sessions
Request approved actions from the website blocker, timer or Notion integration

The agent does not receive unrestricted control over the computer.

Actions such as blocking a website, changing a Notion task or postponing a schedule require user approval. Timers, animations, activity calculations and user-configured focus rules can operate automatically.

Privacy and Safety

Pawse is designed around minimal data collection.

Privacy principles
Activity analysis happens locally whenever possible.
The system records domains rather than complete browsing content.
It counts keyboard activity but never records typed text.
Incognito browsing is excluded.
Users can pause all tracking.
Users can inspect, export or delete their history.
Notion access is limited to selected pages.
Authentication tokens are encrypted.
Medication records are stored securely.
Personal information is never used for advertising.
Health and productivity data are not shared by default.
Safety limits

Pawse never:

Diagnoses mental or physical conditions
Recommends medication or dosage
Confirms medication without user action
Sends messages without permission
Submits assignments
Deletes files
Makes purchases
Shares browsing or wellness history
Uses shame to force productivity

What Makes Pawse Unique

Pawse is not simply:

A Pomodoro timer
A website blocker
A Notion task manager
A wellness reminder
A virtual pet

Its main innovation is connecting all of these components into one meaningful cycle:

Real commitments are discovered from the user’s existing workspace, transformed into playful adventures, protected through context-aware attention tools and rewarded through the growth of a virtual pixel companion.

The system helps users work, rest and recover from distraction without shame. The cat functions as both an emotional companion and an active guardian of the user’s digital environment.

Final Project Description

Pawse is a privacy-first agentic pixel cat designed to improve everyday digital wellness. It lives in the corner of the user’s screen, provides contextual water, stretch, eye-rest and medication reminders, and supports focused work through customizable timers and task checklists. By connecting to user-approved Notion pages and assignment files, Pawse can discover deadlines and requirements and transform them into interactive pixel adventures. During each adventure, its Attention Gatekeeper hides distracting page elements, limits unintentional scrolling and protects the user’s focus without completely blocking useful websites. Completing real tasks and wellness activities earns food, furniture, outfits and story progress for the pet. Pawse combines productivity, self-care, gentle digital-boundary management and virtual-pet companionship without medical diagnosis, punitive streaks or invasive monitoring.
